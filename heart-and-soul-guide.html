Getting Started

You've booted a modded FireRed/Emerald hybrid that plays like an all-star cut of Gold & Silver: the full Johto → Kanto journey, sixteen gyms, and a final climb to Red atop Mt. Silver — but every roster has been dragged into the modern era. Leaders field six-Pokémon teams with held items, competitive movesets, and cross-generation evolutions (Kleavor, Ursaluna, Annihilape, Wyrdeer, Kingdra). This guide walks the whole region in order, tells you what's in the grass, and hands you the battle plans.

Choosing a Starter

Professor Elm's lab starts you with the classic Johto trio — Chikorita, Cyndaquil, or Totodile. There's no wrong pick, but the road ahead skews the odds: Cyndaquil has the smoothest early game (Falkner is trivial, Bugsy folds, and Fire coverage matters for the Bug/Grass/Ice/Steel gyms that dominate Johto). Totodile is the safest all-rounder with the best raw stats. Chikorita is the hard-mode run — it's walled by four of the first six leaders — but a Meganium that survives becomes a superb bulky pivot.

Catch early, catch often

This hack front-loads elite catches. Cherrygrove's grass can drop an Eevee line; Sprout Tower hides Gastly; the National Park has wild Scyther. A great mon caught at level 6 is worth more than any TM.

Mind the day/night clock

Spawns rotate with the in-game time. Nocturnal grass swaps in Hoothoot, Zubat, and the Gastly line; daytime favors birds and normals. If you can't find something, change the hour.

Build coverage, not a monotype

Leaders run six-mon teams with held berries and setup moves. A balanced squad with answers to Steel, Ice, Ground, and Dragon will carry you further than a single overleveled ace.

New Bark Town

Where it all begins. Professor Elm hands you your starter and sends you north to Mr. Pokémon's house on an errand that quietly kicks off the whole adventure. There's nothing in the town grass itself, but the water is worth remembering for later: once you have Surf, the bay off New Bark hides Tentacool, and a rod after dark pulls up Chinchou and even Lanturn — a strong, unusual early Water/Electric type if you circle back. Heal up, save, and step out into Route 29.

Route 29

Your first taste of tall grass, and a surprisingly rich one. By day it's a bird-and-mammal nursery — Pidgey, Sentret, Hoothoot, and Rattata — but the slot table also rolls genuine keepers: Pichu (a future Light Ball Raichu), Marill and its baby Azurill, Hoppip, and Taillow. Grab a Sentret or Pidgey as a disposable HM mule and grind a level or two here; the road only gets steeper. Rock Smash trees shake loose Ledyba and Spinarak.

Cherrygrove City

On paper a sleepy tutorial town where a kind old man walks you around the map. In practice, the single best early-game catch spot in the whole hack. The grass around Cherrygrove can spawn Eevee — and, rarely, a fully-evolved Espeon or Umbreon outright. An Eevee this early is a run-defining companion: evolve it into Espeon (day, high friendship) for a monstrous special attacker, or Umbreon (night) for a wall that trivializes the Ghost and Psychic gyms ahead. Park here, save your game, and hunt the grass until one appears — it's worth every minute.

Dark Cave

An optional pitch-black cavern (bring Flash) that spills off Routes 31 and 45. Early on it's Geodude and Zubat fodder, but its level range scales surprisingly high — deeper visits turn up Ursaring, Teddiursa, Graveler, a rare Dunsparce, and even Wobbuffet. The Surf and fishing spots inside hold Slowpoke and Seel. Not required, but a good detour for a bulky Normal-type or a Dunsparce novelty.

Violet City

Your first gym city. Before you challenge Falkner, clear out Sprout Tower to the north for an easy TM and a stack of Sage battles that'll level your team nicely. The city pond holds nothing but Magikarp — but patience turns one into a Gyarados that single-handedly carries the entire Johto arc. Stock up, then take the Zephyr Badge.

Sprout Tower

A three-storey pagoda whose swaying central pillar is pure atmosphere. Every floor is monks throwing Bellsprout at you — trivial — but the reason to climb is the rare wild Gastly that haunts the tower. A Ghost-type this early is a cheat code: it's completely immune to Normal and Fighting, which walls half of Johto's early trainers, and it grows into a devastating Gengar. Snag one before you leave.

The Unown puzzle ruins — four chambers, each sealed behind a sliding-tile picture puzzle. Solving them fills your Unown "dex" and drops useful items, and the research center up top rewards your progress. Wild Unown flood the interior once you crack a puzzle. It's more lore and completionism than raw power (Unown only knows Hidden Power), but it's a charming, self-contained diversion right on the path to Union Cave.

Your first proper dungeon. Geodude, Zubat, Onix, and a chance at Sandshrew fill the tunnels, with Wooper and Rattata lower down. Goldenrod City

Johto's booming metropolis and the best shopping in the region. The Department Store is your one-stop shop — stock up on healing and raid the TM floor, because the coverage moves here (Thunderbolt, Ice Beam-tier picks) shape your entire mid-game. Grab the free Bicycle from the Bike Shop and the Coin Case for the Game Corner. Other stops: the Radio Tower (a later Rocket target, and where you earn the Radio Card), the Name Rater, a friendship-checker, and a house that gifts an Eevee if you missed the Cherrygrove one. Then there's the gym — Whitney and her infamous Miltank, the wall that ends more runs than any Johto leader. Come prepared.

A gated nature preserve north of Goldenrod, and the site of the Bug-Catching Contest. Pinsir, Nidoran, and a rare Sunkern round out the grass. Even the surf and fishing here are decent. Win the contest for prizes and a shot at rare bugs.

Ecruteak City

The historic, spiritual heart of Johto — all wooden architecture and old legends. Two great towers frame the town: the scorched Burned Tower and the sacred Tin (Bell) Tower. The Dance Theatre hosts the five Kimono Girls, whose performances tie into the roaming-legendary and Lugia/Ho-Oh storylines — beat all five for a reward and a story flag. Keep an eye out for Eusine, the cloak-wearing Suicune fanatic (Politoed, Electrode, Hypno) who dogs your steps around the beast plot. Morty's Ghost gym is the challenge — bring a Dark-type.

Olivine City

A working port dominated by the Glitter Lighthouse, where a gravely ill Ampharos ("Amphy") powers the beacon — and gates the story. Mt. Silver, Red & the Post-Game

The true summit and everything past the credits. Mt. Silver is a level-60s dungeon; the man at the top is level 75–93. Beyond him lie Cerulean Cave's Mewtwo, a Bagon-bearing tower, and the secret starter island — the game's deepest catches for a fully-built team.

Mt. Silver

The endgame mountain — a brutal, sprawling cave-and-cliff complex that only opens after you've beaten the Champion. Wild levels rocket into the 60s: Golem, Ursaring, Skarmory, Magmar, Golbat, and Rapidash roam the grass, the Surf pools hide Lapras and Seaking, and deep chambers hold the Larvitar line (your own future Tyranitar). Bring max-level mons, Repels, and healing — and at the frozen summit, the final trainer waits in silence.

Cerulean Cave

The post-game superdungeon beneath Cerulean — the lair of Mewtwo, sealed until you've earned the region's respect. Everything inside sits at levels 46–67: Ditto, Golbat/Crobat, Kadabra, Electrode, Parasect, and Machoke in the tunnels, wild Slowking and Gyarados in the flooded lower floor. Only enter with a fully-built, fully-healed team and your best ball reserved for the psychic at the bottom.

Embedded Tower

An optional expansion-era tower dungeon with a distinctly cosmic wild list — Solrock and Lunatone patrol the floors alongside Golbat and Crobat, and the real draw is a wild Bagon (the Salamence line) plus Barboach/Whiscash in the interior water. A pseudo-legendary you can raise from scratch makes this worth the climb for team-builders.

Faraway Island

A tiny secret islet that functions as a baby-and-starter garden — the grass here quietly holds Mudkip, Torchic, and Treecko (the Hoenn starters!) plus a nest of babies: Elekid, Smoochum, Azurill, Pichu, and Wynaut. It's a team-builder's paradise and the game's answer to "where do I get starters I didn't pick." Guard your Poké Balls; these don't appear anywhere else.

Contest Hall

A coastal facility better known for its events than its wildlife, but the surrounding water is fishable: Wingull, Tentacool, Staryu, and Slowpoke off the pier, with Magikarp and Seel deeper. A pleasant stop-off rather than a destination — cast a line while you're passing through.

Southern Island

A small, atmospheric island tied to the roaming Eon-Pokémon lore of the expansion base. Its encounter data is sparse — treat it as a scenic, event-driven stop rather than a farming ground. Worth visiting for the set-piece it hosts rather than anything in the grass.

The Champion of Mt. Silver

Red

The hardest fight in the game. Red opens with a Light Ball Pikachu that carries Surf, then rolls out the Kanto starters and Espeon at levels 75–93. There is no type he can't answer. Come at level 80+, pack Full Restores, and lead with something that can eat an Electric hit — a Ground-type neuters his Pikachu entirely.
