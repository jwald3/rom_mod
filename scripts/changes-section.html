What's Changed from Vanilla

Heart & Soul keeps the shape of Johto and Kanto but rewrites a handful of the rules — mostly so you never have to trade to complete an evolution, and so the back half of the game actually fights back. If you've played HG/SS, these are the house rules worth knowing before you start.

◆Read from the ROM

The evolution lists below are pulled straight out of this build's gEvolutions table, and the vitamin prices out of the item table — so they match exactly what the cartridge does, not what a design doc intended.

Trade evolutions are gone — everything evolves solo

No link cable, no second console. The two classic trade paths were both rewritten so a single player can finish every line.

Trade-item lines → use the item

The species that used to need a held item and a trade now evolve the moment you use that same item from the bag, exactly like an Everstone or a Fire Stone. The item is unchanged — only the trade requirement is dropped.

The four Pokémon that evolved on a bare trade now evolve by levelling, at the levels below — no item, no trade.

✦Bag-usable evolution items

Because those items now trigger evolutions directly, every one of them — Metal Coat, King's Rock, Dragon Scale, Up-Grade, DeepSeaTooth, DeepSeaScale — is usable straight from your bag. In vanilla several of these did nothing when used; here they behave like stones.

Other rule changes

₽

Vitamins cost full price

Hp Up, Protein, Iron, Carbos, Calcium, Zinc are priced at the standard ₽9,800 each (they were mistakenly discounted to ₽1,350 in an earlier build). Sell value is half, ₽4,900.

TM

Earthquake moved off Brock

TM26 now teaches Earthquake, and it's a floor pickup in the Rocket Hideout B3F (floor item ball) rather than a gym reward. Brock hands out TM39 (Rock Tomb) instead, so the strongest Ground TM is something you earn by exploring mid-game.

↑

Kanto fights at post-game levels

Kanto is badges 9–16 here, so its wild Pokémon were scaled up from their old Gen-1 levels into a Lv 45–58 band, and any line that would have evolved by then is promoted to match. Slots already above Lv 34 (Cerulean Cave, the deep Seafoam/Cinnabar routes) were left alone.

✕

Roster held to Gen 1–4

Species and moves introduced after Gen 4 were pulled from every trainer team, evolution and wild slot, so nothing in a normal run will fight or become one of them — including Sylveon, Annihilape, Wyrdeer, Ursaluna, Kleavor, Farigiraf, Dudunsparc, Regieleki, Regidrago. The fairy-era moves Play Rough and Moonblast were removed too; this is the no-fairy build.

◈

Starters back to their real types

An earlier build had bolted a second type onto several Pokémon that are single-type in the games — most obviously the Johto starters. Those are reverted: Meganium back to pure Grass, Typhlosion back to pure Fire, Feraligatr back to pure Water, Grovyle back to pure Grass and Sceptile back to pure Grass. A few others lost an invented second type as well (Electivire −Fighting, Golduck −Psychic, Gulpin −Normal, Kingler −Steel, Stantler −Psychic, Sunflora −Fire, Swalot −Normal). Two dual-types were also corrected to their canon pairing — Noctowl to Normal/Flying and Parasect to Bug/Grass.
